# C5 — Civilizational Memory and the Legendary Reconstruction Route

> Fictional simulation/game-design layer. It does not estimate real historical probabilities.

## Core idea

Purrtocol Civilization does not use a flat technology tree. A civilization may know that a machine once existed and still be unable to reproduce it.

A usable capability requires a living dependency chain:

`memory -> tools -> metrology -> materials -> machine tools -> power -> education -> standards -> logistics -> precision industry -> computing -> propulsion -> orbital systems`

The graph is deliberately coupled rather than strictly linear. Losing schools, standards, machine tools, logistics, or material access can erase practical capability while stories and blueprints survive.

## Legendary-route contract

The reconstruction-to-orbit route is intentionally rare.

A 64-seed synthetic balance fixture must produce **at least one but no more than two** escape-ready worlds. The current target therefore makes the route visible but exceptional; this is game calibration, not a claim about real-world history.

Success is **not** required for a valid run. Failure to reach orbit is not a game-over state.

The interesting outcomes are often partial:

- an archive survives but no machine-tool base exists;
- metrology survives as ritual without industrial use;
- propulsion theory becomes scripture;
- a precision-industry island survives inside an otherwise stagnant society;
- education expands but standards fragment;
- abundant resources exist but logistics or peace fail;
- entertainment or elite capture consumes the surplus needed to preserve fragile high technology;
- a civilization repeatedly climbs and falls without ever closing the dependency chain.

## Why knowledge alone is insufficient

`Blueprint != capability.`

A spacecraft blueprint presupposes thousands of mundane capabilities: calibrated measurement, compatible fasteners, reproducible alloys, power quality, machine-tool accuracy, logistics, technical education, shared standards, maintenance culture, and social continuity.

The simulation therefore tracks five persistent continuity conditions:

- archive quality
- resource access
- coordination
- peace capacity
- surplus

They do not directly trigger success. They alter the environment in which the dependency chain either remains alive or decays.

## Replayability

Different seeds receive different small disturbances across hundreds of generations. Because later capabilities depend on earlier ones, small differences can compound into very different histories.

A rare successful seed is not a privileged canonical world. It is one branch among many possible histories.

## Player role

The weak-oracle player cannot choose `BUILD SPACESHIP`.

At most, a revelation might indirectly encourage ideas such as preservation, measurement, cooperation, or curiosity. Whether those ideas survive, become a religion, are monopolized by elites, are laughed at, or eventually support engineering remains an emergent historical process.

## World laws

- `Blueprint != capability.`
- `Knowledge != industrial base.`
- `One missing dependency can strand many downstream capabilities.`
- `Technological memory can survive as myth after practical capability is gone.`
- `Escape route is intentionally rare.`
- `Escape failure != invalid civilization.`
- `Rare success != canonical ending.`
- `NO FINAL TECHNOLOGY.`
- `NO FINAL CIVILIZATION.`

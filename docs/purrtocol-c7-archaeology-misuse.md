# Purrtocol Civilization C7 — Archaeology, Misinterpretation, and Relic Reuse

> **Relic != understood technology.**
>
> **Wrong interpretation != useless interpretation.**

C7 extends Purrtocol Civilization beyond civilizational reconstruction and collapse.
A successor culture may inherit physical artefacts from a civilization that once had
orbital capability while lacking the schools, standards, tools, language continuity,
metrology, and industrial dependencies needed to understand those artefacts.

This is fictional simulation and game-system design. It is not a claim about any
specific real historical culture.

## Design goal

The fun is not only that future Purrtocols get the past wrong. The stronger mechanic is:

```text
advanced artefact
  -> semantic memory is lost
  -> physical affordance survives
  -> successor culture invents an explanation
  -> explanation may be wrong
  -> local use may still work
  -> local success creates an institution
  -> institution changes the next civilization
```

A wrong explanation can therefore be historically false and still become socially,
economically, or technically productive.

## Reference relic field

C7 starts from the legendary C5 advanced civilization and exposes later successor
cultures to a bounded relic field:

- crew capsule,
- engine bell,
- star tracker,
- thermal tiles,
- pressure tank,
- docking ring.

Each relic keeps its original function and physical affordances in the ledger.
Successor cultures do not get that ledger in-world.

## Successor knowledge state

A successor culture receives bounded values for:

- language continuity,
- archive literacy,
- metrology,
- physics,
- materials knowledge,
- machine tools,
- ritualization,
- status pressure,
- scarcity,
- institutional trust.

A blueprint fragment or surviving object cannot by itself reconstruct the original
capability. Correct reconstruction requires a surviving dependency chain.

## Typical misreadings

The reference model permits interpretations such as:

- crew capsule -> royal ancestor tomb / storm refuge / grain vault,
- engine bell -> temple horn / rain catcher / communal furnace hood,
- star tracker -> sky oracle / farmers' calendar box / navigation shrine,
- thermal tiles -> sacred cookstones / fortune-game tiles / priestly roof material,
- pressure tank -> fermentation vat / water cistern / echo temple,
- docking ring -> marriage covenant ring / market measure gate / founding arch.

These are not scripted historical outcomes. They are bounded candidate interpretations
selected by successor conditions.

## Wrong but useful

C7 explicitly preserves **functional echoes**.

Examples:

- a star tracker treated as a sky oracle can preserve seasonal astronomy;
- a docking ring used as a sacred market gate can preserve a dimensional standard;
- thermal protection material used as cookstone can preserve empirical heat-material knowledge;
- a sealed pressure vessel reused as a cistern can preserve sealing craft;
- regular thermal tiles used as game pieces can seed counting notation.

This creates a critical world law:

**Correct theory is not required for every useful practice.**

But useful practice is also not proof of correct theory.

## Confidence is not correctness

Successor social confidence is driven partly by institutional trust, ritualization,
and status pressure. Therefore a culture may be highly confident in an interpretation
that is completely wrong.

The historical ledger retains both:

- original function,
- successor interpretation.

The two must never be silently collapsed.

## Preservation paradox

Misinterpretation can either destroy or preserve evidence.

### Destructive reuse

High scarcity can produce component stripping. A relic may solve a short-term material
problem while permanently erasing information needed by future archaeologists.

### Ritual preservation

A completely false sacred interpretation may prohibit disassembly and accidentally
preserve the artefact for centuries.

A royal tomb may therefore conserve a crew capsule better than a technically curious
but resource-starved workshop.

## Institutions born from mistakes

C7 allows misread relics to become institutions without converting them into correct
technology.

Examples include:

- a sky-oracle calendar priesthood,
- a sacred dimensional standard enforced through a market gate,
- a thermal-tile fortune league,
- relic cults that prohibit destructive inspection.

Later civilizations can inherit these institutions even after the original artefact is
lost.

## Balance contract

The deterministic reference bank uses 24 successor cultures over the same relic field.
It requires:

- original technical reconstruction to remain rare,
- at least 10 distinct successor interpretations,
- multiple highly confident but historically wrong readings,
- multiple wrong-but-useful successor cultures,
- at least one case of ritual preservation,
- at least one case of scarcity-driven stripping.

This is a gameplay balance contract, not a real-world probability estimate.

## World laws

1. `Relic != understood technology`.
2. `Wrong interpretation != useless interpretation`.
3. `Social confidence != historical correctness`.
4. `Ritual preservation can conserve evidence`.
5. `Scarcity-driven reuse can erase evidence`.
6. `Physical affordance can outlive semantic memory`.
7. `Technical reconstruction still requires the dependency chain`.
8. `NO FINAL TECHNOLOGY`.
9. `NO FINAL CIVILIZATION`.

The intended player reaction is not merely:

> "They forgot what the spaceship was."

It is:

> "Why is the docking ring now the legally mandated wedding altar and also the national standard of length?"

That absurdity must remain traceable to the artefact's physical history rather than
being injected as an arbitrary joke event.

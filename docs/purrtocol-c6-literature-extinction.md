# C6 — Literature Ecology, Strange Extinction, and the Lunar Postscript

Status: **fictional simulation / game-system design**.

C6 asks three questions:

1. What kinds of stories become valuable under radically different material conditions?
2. Can a civilization go absolutely extinct in a way that is funny on the surface but mechanically legible underneath?
3. If a legendary civilization reaches the Moon, what prevents the simulation from treating that as a final victory?

## Literature is another ecology

C4 already established that low material budget does not imply low cultural output. C6 projects that result into literature.

The model does **not** say deprivation is good for art. It says different constraints create different selection pressures.

### Labor and debtor niches

Reference genres include:

- `boss-defying-workplace-comedy`
- `worker-hero-wish-fulfilment-serial`
- `stick-fall-neighbourhood-chronicle`
- `impossible-island-escape-romance`
- `tunnel-breakout-fantasy`
- `echo-cosmos-oral-epic`
- `debt-devil-satire`
- `after-shift-invincible-hero`

These niches favor cheap transmission, catharsis, exaggerated reversals, portable fantasy, oral retelling, and jokes that can survive without expensive production infrastructure.

### Elite niches

Reference genres include:

- `dynastic-legitimacy-epic`
- `salon-ambiguity-novel`
- `ennui-microtragedy`
- `aestheticized-poverty-romance`

Elite abundance can shift selection pressure away from material access and toward **status differentiation**: rarity, interpretive difficulty, patronage networks, scarce editions, and ownership of cultural intermediaries.

This can produce a deliberately absurd cycle:

`lower-class hit -> elite discovery -> luxury repackage -> canonization -> moral panic -> private collection`

The same work can be publicly condemned as vulgar and privately collected as authentic.

World boundary:

> Cultural ingenuity is not evidence that deprivation is desirable.

## Strange absolute extinction

Absolute extinction is valid history, but C6 forbids a direct random `EXTINCT` draw.

A world can disappear only after a traceable multi-step chain crosses from culture into institutions and then into infrastructure.

Reference modes include:

### Alarm Festivalization

`safety drill -> spectacle -> performative alarm culture -> alarm distrust -> real warning ignored -> irreversible life-support cascade`

### Museum Spare Taboo

`old machine -> sacred heritage -> cannibalization ban -> spare shortage -> protected exhibit contains needed part -> repair capability loses race against failure`

### Prestige Thermal Overload

`elite prestige -> thermal sculpture infrastructure -> radiator margin consumed -> shutdown becomes culturally costly -> ordinary heat event crosses margin -> habitat cascade`

### Stick-Fall Governance

`street game -> symbol of fairness -> labor-allocation ritual -> critical shifts decouple from skill -> metrology/repair fragmentation -> coordinated maintenance fails`

The surface event may look ridiculous. The terminal mechanism must remain mundane and auditable.

> Funny cause label != magical cause.

## Extinction rarity

The reference bank uses 128 deterministic seeds. Extinction must remain uncommon and diverse. The current gate requires:

- at least 2 and at most 10 absolute extinctions;
- at least 3 distinct extinction modes;
- every extinction must carry a causal chain of at least 4 links.

This is game balancing, not a real-world probability estimate.

## The Moon is not an ending

C5 makes successful reconstruction legendary. C6 takes any C5 escape-ready world and immediately gives it another habitat problem.

A lunar settlement is evaluated against:

- water;
- nitrogen / volatile inventory;
- closed-loop recycling;
- spare-parts capability;
- Earth resupply reliability;
- thermal margin;
- radiation margin.

A civilization can therefore achieve the almost impossible feat of reaching the Moon and discover that it has merely unlocked a new resource trap.

Possible states:

- `LUNAR_RESOURCE_SIEGE`
- `FRAGILE_LUNAR_DEPENDENCY`
- `LOCALLY_ROBUST_LUNAR_HABITAT`

The reference contract explicitly rejects an automatic comfortable ending for the legendary route.

## Game consequence

The player remains a weak oracle, not a civilization controller.

They may watch:

- a debtor-class comedy become a civilization-defining classic;
- elites repackage the same story as an expensive collector object;
- a safety ritual become entertainment;
- a museum law unexpectedly become part of an extinction chain;
- a civilization reach the Moon and immediately enter ration politics.

The interesting question is not only *what happened?* but:

> **Why did this ridiculous thing become causally important?**

## World laws

- `Low resource budget != low expression.`
- `Cultural ingenuity != justification for deprivation.`
- `Elite abundance can select for status differentiation.`
- `Popular culture can cross class boundaries and change meaning.`
- `Absolute extinction is valid history.`
- `Extinction != random event card.`
- `Weird extinction requires auditable causality.`
- `Reaching the Moon != victory.`
- `New habitat == new dependencies.`
- `NO FINAL CULTURE.`
- `NO FINAL TECHNOLOGY.`
- `NO FINAL CIVILIZATION.`
